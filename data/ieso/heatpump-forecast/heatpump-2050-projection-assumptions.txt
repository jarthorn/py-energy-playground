PROJECTION: INCREMENTAL ONTARIO ELECTRICITY DEMAND IF ALL HOUSEHOLDS SWITCH TO
HEAT PUMPS BY 2050 (vs. the IESO baseline of 6.3% of households)
===============================================================================

Question: How much additional electricity would Ontario need in 2050 if EVERY
household used a heat pump for space + water heating, instead of the IESO 2025/
2026 Annual Planning Outlook reference assumption that only ~6.3% of households
do?

Headline result (base case):
  ~18-19 TWh per year of INCREMENTAL electricity demand
  (full 100% conversion of today's residential gas heating = ~19.6 TWh;
   subtracting the 6.3% already electrified in the baseline = ~18.4 TWh).

Plausible range (see sensitivity below): ~14 TWh to ~33 TWh per year.

For context, the IESO 2026 APO reference forecast puts total provincial net
demand at ~250 TWh and the entire residential sector at ~59 TWh in 2050. So this
single change would add roughly 7-8% to total provincial demand and would be on
the order of +30% on top of the residential sector forecast.

Output data table: heatpump-2050-projection.csv
Model script:      bin/heatpump_projection.py


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Delivered / useful-energy basis, applied separately to space and water heating:

    useful_heat    = gas_energy_thermal x gas_appliance_efficiency
    hp_electricity = useful_heat / heat_pump_seasonal_COP

Incremental over baseline = hp_electricity x (1 - 0.063).

Natural-gas energy is taken directly from Statistics Canada deliveries reported
in gigajoules (GJ), which are on a higher-heating-value (HHV) basis. Furnace AFUE
and water-heater energy factors are also defined on an HHV basis, so the two are
consistent and NO separate heating-value (MJ/m3) assumption is required.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DATA SOURCES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Residential natural gas consumption -- Statistics Canada Table 25-10-0059-01
   ("Canadian monthly natural gas distribution"), Ontario, "Deliveries to
   residential consumers." Annual totals (derived in
   ontario-residential-natgas-by-year.csv):
       2023: 82.9 TWh   2024: 79.2 TWh   2025: 90.5 TWh
       10-yr (2016-2025) average: 87.7 TWh thermal  <-- base-case input
   (Cross-check: Canada Energy Regulator reports Ontario residential gas use of
   ~0.7 Bcf/d in 2023, i.e. ~7.2 billion m3/yr ~ 83 TWh -- consistent.)

2. Households / gas-heating share -- Natural Resources Canada (2021): Ontario had
   ~5.72 million households, of which ~4.05 million (70.9%) heat primarily with
   natural gas. StatCan reports ~3.6 million residential gas customer accounts
   (2024). Heat-pump share today is ~2.5% of households (IESO).

3. Heat-pump efficiency -- ENERGY STAR Most Efficient 2025 cold-climate criteria
   (HSPF2 >= 8.5; COP >= 1.75 at 5 degF). Best current cold-climate models reach
   HSPF2 ~10-14 (~COP 2.9-4.1 at rating conditions); realistic seasonal COP in
   Ontario's climate today is ~2.5-3.0.

4. Baseline / context -- IESO 2025 & 2026 Annual Planning Outlook (6.3% household
   heat-pump adoption by 2050; ~250 TWh total and ~59 TWh residential in 2050).

5. 2050 population/households -- Ontario Ministry of Finance projections
   (16.1M in 2024 -> ~20.5M by 2051).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KEY ASSUMPTIONS (and how to change them)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. ALL residential natural gas is space or water heating. (Per the request.
   In reality a few % is cooking/other, so this slightly overstates heating.)

B. Base-case gas load = 87.7 TWh thermal/yr (10-year average, to normalize for
   weather). Using a single recent year would give 79-91 TWh.

C. Space : water split of residential gas = 70 : 30.

D. Average existing gas-appliance efficiency:
       Space (furnace AFUE) = 0.85   (stock blend of mid- + high-efficiency)
       Water (energy factor) = 0.63  (typical storage gas water heater)
   -> blended useful-heat fraction ~0.78; useful heat ~68.8 TWh.

E. 2050 cold-climate heat-pump SEASONAL COP = 3.5 (base case). This deliberately
   EXCEEDS today's best realistic seasonal COP (~3.0), per the request that 2050
   average efficiency surpass the present maximum. Sensitivity run over 2.5-4.5.
   (Same COP applied to space and water; heat-pump water heaters today already
   achieve COP ~3-3.5, so this is reasonable for both.)

F. Incremental-over-baseline treats the IESO 6.3% household heat-pump share as
   6.3% of the heating load already electrified, so incremental = 93.7% of the
   full-conversion electricity. (Minor effect: ~1 TWh.)

G. "Current gas load" basis holds residential heating demand at today's level.
   A second "scaled to 2050 households" basis multiplies by projected household
   growth (~1.26x, from population growth) to approximate a larger 2050 housing
   stock. This does NOT assume any change in per-home heating intensity (e.g.,
   better building envelopes, which would lower it).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULTS (TWh of incremental electricity per year, 2050)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Basis = today's gas-heating load held constant:
   Seasonal COP:        2.5    3.0    3.5    4.0    4.5
   Full conversion:    27.5   22.9   19.6   17.2   15.3
   Incremental (vs 6.3%):25.8  21.5   18.4   16.1   14.3

Basis = scaled to projected 2050 household count (~1.26x):
   Seasonal COP:        2.5    3.0    3.5    4.0    4.5
   Full conversion:    34.7   28.9   24.8   21.7   19.3
   Incremental (vs 6.3%):32.5  27.1   23.2   20.3   18.1

Base case (current load, COP 3.5): ~18.4 TWh/yr incremental.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IMPORTANT CAVEATS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ENERGY ONLY, not peak/capacity. Full heating electrification would raise the
  WINTER PEAK substantially more than these annual-energy figures suggest,
  because heat-pump efficiency falls and heating load spikes on the coldest days
  (often with electric-resistance backup). Peak impact would need an hourly model.
- No allowance for building-envelope improvements, demand response, hybrid
  (gas-backup) heat pumps, or behavioural change -- each of which would lower the
  result. Conversely, very cold snaps with resistance backup would raise it.
- Uses delivered/seasonal-average efficiencies; a true bottom-up model would use
  temperature-dependent COP curves against Ontario's hourly climate.
- The 6.3% baseline is a share of households; mapping it onto the gas-energy load
  is an approximation.
