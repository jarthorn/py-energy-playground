MAJOR KNOWN ASSUMPTIONS IN THE IESO'S MOST RECENT ONTARIO DEMAND FORECAST
===========================================================================

Source: IESO 2025 Annual Planning Outlook (APO), published April 2025, covering
the outlook period 2026-2050 (reference year 2025). This is the most recent
long-term provincial demand forecast and is the basis for the total and peak
energy demand figures in ieso-2026-demand-forecast.txt. Supplementary detail is
drawn from the IESO's July 2025 demand-side technical papers (Electric Vehicles;
Space Heating Electrification; Large Step Loads) and, for demographics, the
Ontario Ministry of Finance population projections (Summer 2025), which the IESO
relies on as a demographic input.

Note on scope: The APO presents a Reference (most-likely) forecast plus a
High-Demand Scenario and Low-Demand Scenario that "bookend" plausible outcomes.
Unless stated otherwise, the figures below are from the Reference forecast.
The APO does not speculate on future policy: it only incorporates changes that
are expected to result from the latest economic data, committed government
policy, or announced actions. Forecast inputs were finalized in mid-2024, so
later developments (e.g., U.S. tariff threats, the new 2025-2036 eDSM targets)
are NOT reflected and will be captured in future outlooks.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HEADLINE FORECAST ASSUMPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- System-level net annual energy demand grows from ~151 TWh (2025) / 157 TWh
  (2026) to 262 TWh in 2050 -- a 75% increase, vs. the 60% increase forecast in
  the 2024 APO. Compound annual growth rate (CAGR): ~2.2%.
- Net summer peak: grows from ~24 GW (2026) to ~36 GW (2050); +48%, CAGR ~1.7%.
- Net winter peak: grows comparably, producing a dual-peaking system (summer and
  winter peaks of similar magnitude) from ~2030 onward.
- Key growth drivers assumed: industrial EV production / supply-chain step loads;
  AI/cloud data centres; broad economy-wide electrification; and higher
  population growth and household formation.
- Growth-tempering assumptions: higher energy-efficiency (eDSM) program savings,
  softer agricultural-greenhouse growth, higher cost of living, and elevated
  economic uncertainty.
- General macro assumptions: stable electricity rates, and rising natural gas
  rates (including rising GHG/carbon costs over the outlook), which make
  electrification relatively more attractive.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. POPULATION GROWTH
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The APO uses standard demographic projections (Ontario Ministry of Finance) as
  a core driver but does not publish a single headline population number in the
  main report. The reference (medium-growth) demographic basis is:
    * Ontario population ~16.1 million (mid-2024) growing to >20.5 million by
      2051 -- an increase of ~4.4 million, or +27.4% over 27 years.
    * Low-growth demographic scenario: ~18.1 million by 2051 (+12.2%, +2.0M).
    * High-growth demographic scenario: ~23.2 million by 2051 (+43.7%, +7.0M).
- Population growth is assumed to be driven mainly by national immigration policy,
  tempered by interprovincial out-migration.
- The 2025 APO assumes HIGHER population growth and household formation than the
  2024 APO -- a key reason demand growth was revised upward (60% -> 75%).
- Demographics (population change, geographic migration, household type shares)
  are explicitly flagged as a source of forecast uncertainty.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOUSEHOLDS / RESIDENTIAL SECTOR
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Household count forecasts were updated upward and assume increased
  concentration of homes in urban areas, notably the Toronto and Ottawa zones.
- Continued new-home construction is assumed, supported by a favourable
  provincial housing-supply policy environment.
- Persisting full- and hybrid work-from-home trends are assumed, raising daytime
  household occupancy and therefore residential electricity use.
- Continued growth in adoption of electronic appliances/devices.
- Toronto Green Standard v6 (planned 2028) is assumed to require near-zero-
  emissions buildings constructed in Toronto from 2030 -- pushing electrification
  in new residential (and commercial) construction.
- Residential building-type shares were re-calibrated.
- Overall residential net annual energy demand: 51 TWh (2026) -> 63 TWh (2050),
  +25%, CAGR ~0.9%.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HEAT PUMPS -- SPACE AND WATER HEATING ELECTRIFICATION (GREEN TECH)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Heat pumps are treated as the MAIN technology for electrifying residential and
  commercial space heating and domestic hot water heating.
- Space + domestic hot water heating represents >60% of total residential and
  commercial energy use, so its electrification is one of the largest sources of
  long-term demand uncertainty.
- Heat pump adoption is assumed to remain MODEST in the Reference forecast:
  rising from ~2.5% of households today to ~6.3% of households by 2050.
- This modest adoption assumes incentive programs that have concluded or are
  winding down, specifically:
    * Canada Greener Homes Grant (concluded)
    * Ontario Clean Home Heating Initiative (concluded)
  with continued (smaller) uptake driven by:
    * Toronto Green Standard
    * "natural" market uptake / equipment replacement
    * Canada Greener Homes Affordability program
    * Natural Resources Canada Oil-to-Heat-Pump Affordability program
- Heat pumps are assumed to consume less energy than traditional electric-
  resistance heating.
- Key acknowledged uncertainties around heat pumps:
    * Pace of adoption,
    * Variability in heat-pump operation (cold-climate performance and
      temperature-dependent coefficient of performance), and
    * Government policy.
- Electrified heating is assumed to increase WINTER load especially, making the
  system more weather/climate sensitive; if non-AC households adopt heat pumps,
  summer load also rises.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ELECTRIC VEHICLES -- LIGHT-DUTY (GREEN TECH)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Starting point: >200,000 EVs registered in Ontario by end-2024 (~2% of all
  vehicles); ~400,000 light-duty EVs assumed in 2025.
- The Reference forecast aligns EV adoption with federal Electric Vehicle
  Availability Standard regulations (published Dec 2023), assuming zero-emission
  vehicles reach the following share of NEW light-duty vehicle sales:
    * >=20% in 2026
    * >=60% in 2030
    * 100% in 2035
- Light-duty EV stock assumed to grow from ~400,000 (2025) to ~8.1 million (2040)
  to ~11.5 million (2050). (The EV technical paper cites ~11.6 million total EVs
  by 2050.)
- Adoption is assumed to be reinforced by policy measures, improved technology,
  matured production, and shifting consumer preference away from internal-
  combustion vehicles.
- A "slow adoption" alternative (technical paper, NOT the APO Reference) assumes
  only ~75% of light-duty sales are battery-electric by 2035, delaying the 100%
  milestone by ~8 years (to ~2043).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ELECTRIC VEHICLES -- MEDIUM- AND HEAVY-DUTY (GREEN TECH)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Medium- and heavy-duty vehicles (>4,536 kg) are currently <3% of total
  provincial vehicles.
- Battery-electric adoption in this segment is assumed to LAG light-duty EVs,
  reaching ~90,000 units by 2050 -- about 23% of total medium-/heavy-duty
  vehicles.
- Aligns with federal targets for a subset of medium-/heavy-duty vehicles to be
  zero-emission by 2040.
- No electricity demand is assumed for non-battery decarbonization technologies
  (hydrogen fuel cell, catenary/overhead, bio-based diesel, etc.); their future
  market shares are treated as uncertain.
- Electrification of this segment is assumed to be more challenging because these
  vehicles are operated almost exclusively for business with diverse, demanding
  usage patterns.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EV CHARGING ASSUMPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Total EV charging demand assumed to exceed 42 TWh/year by 2050 (~a sixty-fold
  increase over the period) -- about 16% of total provincial electricity
  consumption, making EV charging the single LARGEST electricity end use by 2050.
- The transportation sector overall grows from ~3 TWh (2026) to ~44 TWh (2050)
  (+1,382%, CAGR ~11.9%), the fastest-growing sector, also including rail/transit
  electrification (GO rail, LRT, subway projects).
- A MODELLED charging profile assuming continued charging-management initiatives
  is used; charging behaviour is highly uncertain and influenced by rate
  structures (e.g., Ultra-Low Overnight rates) and managed-charging programs.
- Winter EV battery charging combined with winter electric space heating is a key
  driver of the shift toward winter-evening/overnight peaks and flatter daily
  load profiles.
- Active/managed EV charging is assumed to be increasingly necessary long-term to
  avoid large incremental capacity (peak) needs.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. OTHER RELEVANT ELECTRIFICATION / DEMAND DRIVERS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Industrial EV production & supply chain (e.g., Honda Alliston EV assembly +
  battery plant; LG/Stellantis Windsor; Volkswagen St. Thomas) drive large
  industrial "step loads."
- Commercial data centres (AI/cloud) grow from ~3 TWh (2026) to ~16 TWh (2050)
  (+423%, CAGR ~7.1%) -- a major but highly uncertain new load.
- Industrial decarbonization: steel electrification (Algoma, ArcelorMittal
  Dofasco electric arc furnaces), mining electrification, battery-materials and
  hydrogen production.
- Energy efficiency / demand-side management (eDSM) assumed to offset some growth
  (note: the larger 2025-2036 eDSM targets are NOT yet reflected).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. KEY CAVEATS ON ASSUMPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Forecast inputs finalized mid-2024; excludes later events (U.S. tariffs, new
  eDSM targets, newer policy).
- Only committed/announced policy and projects are included; the APO does not
  speculate on future policy changes.
- Uncertainty grows with the length of the outlook; the largest sources of
  demand uncertainty are: space/water heating electrification (heat pumps), EV
  adoption & charging, large step loads (data centres + EV supply chain), the
  economy, demographics, fuel costs, technology, and climate change.
- Figures are on a normal-weather, net-demand basis (gross demand minus
  conservation; net minus embedded generation = grid demand).
